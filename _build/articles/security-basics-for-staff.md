You do not need to be technical to stop most cyber attacks. Attackers usually go after people, not systems, because one hurried click is easier than breaking a firewall. These habits protect you and your company. Share this page with your team.

## 1. Spot the phishing message

Phishing is a message (email, SMS, WhatsApp, a phone call) that tricks you into clicking a link, opening a file, sharing a code or sending money. Warning signs:

- **Urgency or fear**: "Your account will be closed today", "Pay this invoice now".
- **A request that skips the normal process**: a "director" asking for an urgent bank transfer or gift cards.
- **A sender address that is almost right**: `rnicrosoft.com` (r + n) instead of `microsoft.com`.
- **A link that does not go where it claims.** Hover over it (or long-press on a phone) to see the real address before clicking.
- **An unexpected attachment**, especially compressed files, or documents asking you to "enable content".

**When in doubt, verify by another channel.** Call the person on a number you already know. Never use the contact details in the suspicious message.

## 2. Passwords: long, unique and managed

- Use a **passphrase** of several unrelated words rather than a short complex password.
- **Never reuse** a work password anywhere else. When another website is breached, attackers try the same password on email and banking.
- Use a **password manager** (many are free) so you only need to remember one strong passphrase.
- Never share your password, not even with IT. Real IT staff never need it.

## 3. Turn on multifactor authentication

MFA asks for a second proof, usually an approval in an app, when you sign in. Even if your password is stolen, the attacker cannot get in. If you get an MFA prompt you did not start, **deny it and report it**. Someone has your password. (IT teams: see [turning on MFA in Microsoft 365](../m365-mfa-security-defaults/).)

## 4. Keep devices updated and locked

- Install updates when prompted; most fix security problems that are already being exploited.
- Lock your screen when you step away (**Windows key + L**).
- Do not plug in USB drives you found or were given unexpectedly.
- Use company storage (OneDrive or SharePoint) rather than personal accounts for work files.

## 5. Be careful on public Wi-Fi and with QR codes

Free Wi-Fi in cafés and airports can be watched or faked. Avoid signing in to work systems on it without the company VPN. QR codes can lead to fake login pages just like links can.

## 6. Report fast, without fear

If you clicked something, entered your password on a strange page, or sent information you shouldn't have, **tell IT immediately**. In the first minutes, a password reset or a blocked session can stop a small mistake becoming a company-wide incident. A good IT team thanks people who report; it never punishes them for it.

## One-minute checklist

- [ ] I pause and verify unexpected urgent requests
- [ ] I check where a link really goes before I click
- [ ] My work password is unique and stored in a password manager
- [ ] MFA is on for my email and important accounts
- [ ] I install updates and lock my screen
- [ ] I know how to report a problem to IT
